How We Choose What To Automate

Not everything should be automated. We score candidates on three things.

Volume. If it happens twice a week, automation rarely pays for itself. If it happens forty times a day, it usually does.

Cost of a mistake. Low-stakes, easily checked work is a good first pilot. Anything involving pricing, legal or a customer's money starts with a human in the loop.

Definition of done. If two people would do the task differently, the first job is to standardise it. Automating an unclear process just produces bad output faster.

We start with one workflow, measure the baseline (hours and error rate), then decide whether to extend it. No six-month transformation programme before anything works.

Built With Guardrails

Automation that is safe enough to leave running.

  • Human approval on anything that reaches a customer, unless you explicitly approve full automation.
  • Logging — every automated action is traceable to its input, so failures can be diagnosed.
  • Fallbacks — when a model is uncertain or a system is down, work routes to a person instead of failing silently.
  • Data discipline — customer data only goes to tools you have approved, with retention rules agreed in writing.
  • Measured outcomes — hours saved, response times and error rates tracked before and after.

How An Engagement Runs

One useful pilot beats a roadmap with nothing shipped.

STEP 1

Audit

We map where hours actually go and pick the workflow with the best return.

STEP 2

Pilot

One workflow, connected to real data, running alongside the current process.

STEP 3

Measure

Baseline versus pilot: time saved, error rate, response time.

STEP 4

Harden

Approval steps, logging, fallbacks and documentation for the people who use it.

STEP 5

Expand

Only after the pilot proves itself — usually to the workflow next door.

STEP 6

Review

Quarterly check that the automation still matches how the team works.

Ask a question
Is this replacing our staff?
No. It removes the repetitive part of their week so they can spend time on work that needs judgement — calls, strategy, exceptions. Every project we run has a named human responsible for the output.
Which tools do you build with?
We pick based on what you already pay for and where your data lives. Common stacks are your CRM plus an automation layer and a model API, with reporting in the analytics tool you already use.
How quickly can something be live?
A single workflow pilot is usually live in two to four weeks, including the audit and the approval design. Larger rollouts follow the pilot's measured results.
What about data privacy?
Customer data only goes to tools you approve, with retention and access rules documented. We do not train models on your data, and anything sensitive can be excluded from the workflow entirely.

Which task eats the most hours every week?

No pitch deck, no 40-slide audit. A 30-minute call about what you need and what it takes.

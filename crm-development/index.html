How A CRM Project Runs

Fixed scope, fixed price, and a date you can plan around.

1. Audit (about a week). We sit with the people who use the system, map the real process, and list what's missing, duplicated, or ignored.

2. Data model. Objects, fields, stages and permissions are agreed before anything is built. This is where most CRM projects quietly fail.

3. Build or configure. Pipelines, automation, templates, dashboards and integrations, delivered in short reviewable chunks — not one big reveal.

4. Migrate. De-duplicate, map old fields to new, import, then spot-check records with the team that owns them.

5. Train and hand over. Short sessions by role (sales, admin, management), written notes, and admin access in your name.

6. Thirty-day adjustment. Real usage always surfaces friction. We fix the first round of it at no extra cost.

Ask a question
How long does a CRM project take?
Configuration and clean-up projects typically take one to three weeks. Custom builds run four to ten weeks depending on integrations and how much historical data has to be migrated.
Can you fix our existing CRM instead of replacing it?
Yes — that is most of what we do. If HubSpot, Zoho, Pipedrive or Salesforce can be configured to match your process, replacing it is usually the more expensive option. We only recommend a custom build when the platform genuinely can't express how you sell.
Do we own the data and the code?
Always. The CRM account, the data and any custom code stay in your name. If we part ways, nothing has to be re-platformed and no licence sits with us.
Will you work alongside our developer or IT team?
Yes. We can own the whole build, or deliver the CRM layer while your team handles infrastructure, SSO and internal systems. Access is scoped and documented either way.
